# How much does weather add to bike demand forecasts?

Daily Citi Bike trips in New York, 2023 to 2025 (125 million trips counted per day from 37 monthly files), and daily weather at Central Park. Five questions:
- how well can a month of daily trips be forecast from the past only: last year's value, a calendar regression, gradient boosting;
- how much do temperature, rain and snow add on top of the calendar (an ablation: added alone, and removed from all three);
- what is the weather trap: observed weather on the day is an upper bound, and the honest inputs are the normal weather for the day and last week's weather;
- how big are the errors on holidays and storm days;
- how different are members and casual riders.

The questions follow GeeksforGeeks' "Predictive Modeling for Sales or Demand Forecasting", with Citi Bike trips as the demand series. The page with the results and the charts is the build log:

Build log: https://mrrishit909.github.io/projects/citibike-demand/

## Steps

1. **Download and count.** Each monthly zip is fetched, counted with DuckDB by the local date of `started_at` (total, member, casual, classic, electric, and audit counts), checked against a plain line count of the CSV files, and deleted. The weather file is fetched whole.
2. **Audit.** Headers, bike and rider types, start-time formats, duplicate ride ids, end-before-start rows, long rides, rows that start outside the file's month, days without a count; weather values missing or flagged.
3. **Baselines.** The same weekday a year earlier (day - 364), and that times the growth of the last 28 days over the same 28 days a year earlier.
4. **Walk-forward models.** For each of the 24 test months (2024-01 to 2025-12) every model is fitted on all days before the month (an expanding window, 365 days for the first month) and forecasts all its days, 1 to 31 days ahead, in logs, for total, member and casual trips. Regression: weekday, holiday, two yearly harmonics and a linear trend, plus temperature (mean of TMAX and TMIN, and its square), rain (log(1+mm), a wet day of 1 mm or more) and snow (log(1+mm), snow on the ground). Boosting: scikit-learn GradientBoostingRegressor (300 trees, depth 3, rate 0.05, subsample 0.8, seed 0, no tuning) on weekday, holiday, day of year and the raw weather values, fitted to log trips minus the calendar regression's trend (trees cannot extrapolate growth), the trend added back.
5. **Weather ablation and the weather trap.** The ten configurations of each model: calendar only; temperature, rain or snow added; all three; one group removed from all three; and all three given as the 1993-2022 normal for the day (mean over +-7 days) or as the mean of the last seven days held flat. Models are always fitted on observed weather; only the weather fed in at prediction time changes.
6. **Scores and tests.** Mean absolute percentage error, mean absolute error, root mean squared error and mean signed % error over the 731 test days (all, 2024, 2025); Diebold-Mariano on daily absolute errors (30 Bartlett lags); holidays (federal holidays, the observed day when on a weekend, the day after Thanksgiving), storm days (1 inch of rain or snow) and other days; the 12 worst days; the all-weather regression on all 1,096 days with Newey-West (14 lags) errors; how well the normals and last week know the weather.
7. **Check.** `check.py` recomputes the daily series from the per-month count files, the normals, every regression fit (240 fits by normal equations and Gauss-Jordan elimination, no numpy), baselines, scores, ablation, segments, tests and the Newey-West regressions: 31,158 numbers to a relative tolerance of 1e-8. The boosted forecasts cannot be refitted without the library: they are stored in `results/gbm_predictions.csv`, checked by properties (every test day and target present, finite, positive) and every number that uses them is recomputed from the stored values.
8. **Charts.**

## Results

- **The data.** 125.2 million trips on 1,096 days; no gaps, no duplicate ride id, the same 13 columns, two bike types (classic, electric) and two rider types (member, casual) in all 37 files. Trips per day: 96,184 in 2023, 121,046 in 2024 (+25.8%), 125,400 in 2025 (+3.6%); electric bikes 50%, 66%, 70% of trips; members 81%, 81%, 83%.
- **Last year is a weak forecast.** Mean absolute percentage error over the 731 test days, total trips: same weekday last year 25.2% (bias -5.8%), scaled by recent growth 24.6% (+6.4%), calendar regression 22.6% (+6.4%), calendar boosting 22.7% (+5.3%). The regression beats the scaled naive forecast by 3,292 trips a day (p = 0.004); boosting is not better than the regression (p = 0.09).
- **The level moves.** The regression's trend was learned while trips per day grew 26%; in 2025 growth was 3.6% and the regression's bias is +13.2% (2024: -0.4%). The first test month is fitted on 12 months only, which cannot separate trend from season. Over 2025 alone every calendar-only forecast is 22% to 23% off.
- **Observed weather is an upper bound that cuts the error by 37% to 47%.** Regression 22.6% to 14.2%, boosting 22.7% to 12.0% (p < 0.001 for both).
- **The honest weather inputs add nothing.** Normal weather: regression 21.9% (p = 0.63 against the calendar), boosting 22.6% with a bias of -8.7%. Last week's weather: regression 25.9% (worse than the calendar, p = 0.025), boosting 22.9%. Over the test days the normal weather explains 84% of the variance of the mean temperature, -0.6% of rain and -4.0% of snow (negative values mean no skill), and last week's weather 67%, -9.9% and -2.0%.
- **Temperature and rain each add about as much; snow adds nothing more.** The regression favours rain, boosting temperature. Regression, observed weather, points of error removed when a group is added to the calendar / when it is removed from all three: temperature 3.1 / 3.5, rain 4.2 / 4.9, snow 1.4 / -0.1, all three 8.5. Boosting: temperature 5.9 / 5.0, rain 4.9 / 4.6, snow 1.0 / 0.0, all three 10.7.
- **Holidays and storms.** Regression error: 19% on 678 other days (observed weather 12%), 53% on 24 holidays (47% with normal weather, 40% observed), 92% on 29 storm days, (a bias of +92%: it forecasts about twice the trips), 87% with normal weather and 41% with observed weather. The worst day of the honest regression is 2025-12-27 (12,871 trips, 66 mm of snow; forecast 79,377); 8 of its 12 worst days are holidays or storm days.
- **Members and casual riders.** Regression error, calendar only / normal weather / observed weather: members 21.1% / 20.5% / 13.7%, casual riders 35.2% / 31.9% / 18.1%. In the all-weather regression on all days, 10 to 20 °C raises trips +24% for members (19 to 28) and +62% for casual riders (53 to 71); 10 mm of rain -28% and -38%; a Saturday against a Monday -15% and +60%; a federal holiday -35% and +5% (casual interval -5 to 17).
- **Traps found.** A monthly file holds the trips that end in it: 20,410 rows start in another month, and 31 December 2025 needs the January 2026 file, so trips are counted by start date over all files. All 1,125 rows that end before they start are in the three Novembers (clocks go back). 47,300 rows last 24 hours or more and 1,317 less than 60 seconds or have no end time; none was removed. 69,638 rows have no start station. No schema change inside 2023 to 2025. One snow-depth value is missing (treated as no snow on the ground, as in the normals).

## Not done, and caveats

- Trips taken are not demand: when no bike or dock is free a trip does not happen, and fleet size, stations, docks, pricing, the rise of electric bikes (from 50% to 70% of trips) and system expansion are not modelled (only a linear trend), so the level of every forecast is shaky. The 2023 to 2025 window is short, and one region (the monthly files; Jersey City's are separate and not used) is matched to one weather station (Central Park).
- The honest weather inputs are the normal weather and last week's, not a real weather forecast. A real forecast of the next days was not tested (no archive of forecasts was downloaded); it would sit somewhere between the honest cases and the upper bound for the first days, and by how much is the open question.
- Models are fitted on observed weather and fed an average at prediction time (a plug-in). Fitting on forecast-like inputs, or averaging over weather scenarios, was not tried; boosting's bias of -8.7% with normal weather shows the cost.
- The boosting settings are fixed and untuned; no other model family (e.g. Prophet, ARIMA with regressors, neural networks) was tried. The regression has a log-linear trend and two yearly harmonics; interactions (weekday by weather, the season by the rider type) were not tried, and the first fold has only 12 months of history.
- Errors are mostly percentage errors, which weigh low-ridership days heavily (winter, storms); mean absolute errors and root mean squared errors are in `results/scores.csv`. Intervals in the sensitivity table are in-sample Newey-West intervals for these three years, not causal effects or out-of-sample intervals. The Diebold-Mariano p-values assume 30 lags are enough for forecasts up to 31 days ahead and are not corrected for the number of comparisons.
- Holidays are the federal ones plus the day after Thanksgiving; the marathon, parades, concerts, school terms, subway outages and strikes are not modelled. "Storm" is an inch of rain or snow at Central Park, chosen before the errors were looked at. Weather at the park is not the weather in every borough, and rain on a day's evening differs from rain at the commute.
- 47,300 trips of 24 hours or more and 1,317 very short ones are counted as trips; the daily count is of trips started that day.
